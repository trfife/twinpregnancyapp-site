What relatives should know about buying for twins

Three things twin parents wish everyone knew before the party.

1. Matching vs. individual: ask the parents

Some twin families love matching outfits and identical toys; others work hard to treat each twin as a separate kid with separate tastes. Neither is wrong, but guessing wrong is awkward. Ask, and if you cannot ask, coordinate rather than match: the same item in two colors, or two books from the same series. Never give a gift to one twin and not the other, even a small one.

2. Two of anything that goes in a hand

Under about three, twins do not share, they parallel-play. Anything small enough to hold, chew, push, ride or carry needs to arrive in a pair or it will be fought over within minutes. That includes crayons, stacking rings, music toys, board books and bikes. Clothes and consumables (pajamas, sippy cups, socks) should always come as two, and one size up from what the twins wear today, because they are about to outgrow it.

3. One of anything big enough to stand around

Play kitchens, water tables, block sets, wagons and play tents work as a single shared gift because there is room for two kids at once. These are the presents to pool money on with other relatives, and the ones to check with the parents about first so the family does not end up with two kitchens. Big gifts also take floor space, and twin households are already short on it.
