Second birthday gifts for twins

At two, twins start playing with each other instead of next to each other. That changes what is worth buying twice and what is better as one big thing they build on together.

What changes between one and two

Two-year-old twins can take turns for about four seconds, which is enough to make open-ended shared toys (blocks, a kitchen, a water table) work brilliantly. Anything with a single "driver's seat," like a bike or a crayon, still needs to come in a pair. The rule of thumb: if the toy has more than one place to stand and play, buy one; if it goes in a hand, buy two.

Balance bikes

The gift most two-year-old twins remember. Get two, always.

Strider 12 Classic Balance Bike

The Strider is the balance bike most families end up with because it is light enough (under seven pounds) for a two-year-old to pick up when it falls over, which is constantly. No pedals, no chain, puncture-proof tires, and a seat that drops low enough for a small twenty-four-month-old. Twins who start on these at two are usually gliding by three and pedaling without training wheels not long after.

Buy two? Absolutely, in two different colors. One bike between twins is a guaranteed meltdown, and a pair of Striders rides together at exactly the same pace, which is the whole point.

Play kitchen & pretend play

One big shared piece, plus the things two chefs each need.

Melissa & Doug Wooden Chef's Pretend Play Kitchen

If you buy one big shared gift for two-year-old twins, make it the kitchen. This one is wide enough (about three and a half feet) that two kids can stand at it without elbowing, with a fridge on one end and a stove on the other so each twin has a station. The turning dials, ice dispenser and sink handle survive years of abuse. Note the box says 3+; two-year-olds mostly open doors and bang pots, so keep the small "ice cubes" put away early on.

Buy two? No. Two kitchens is the most common twin-gift mistake. Buy one kitchen and two sets of play food or pots instead.

Dress-up trunk or costume set

Two is when pretend play arrives, and hats, capes and firefighter jackets are cheap fuel for it. Look for sets sized 2T-3T with big hook-and-loop closures the twins can manage themselves, and skip anything with tiny accessories. A trunk of mixed costumes works better for twins than two identical outfits because they can swap, and "you be the doctor, I'll be the dog" is the first real cooperative game most twins play.

Buy two? One trunk with at least two of the favorite piece (two capes, two hats). Single-costume sets should come in pairs.

Big blocks & building

The best shared gifts at two. More pieces beats more sets.

Mega Bloks Big Building Bag (80 pieces)

Oversized, soft-edged blocks that a two-year-old can actually press together. Eighty pieces sounds like a lot until two kids are each building a tower, and then it is about right. The zip bag is the real feature, because cleanup with twins is the parents' problem and this makes it a thirty-second job.

Buy two? Get two bags if the budget allows, not because each twin needs their own but because 160 blocks makes a much better shared pile than 80.

LEGO DUPLO Classic Brick Box (10913)

Smaller than Mega Bloks, so it suits twins with steadier hands, and it is the start of a system that grows with them for years. The 65 pieces include a car, a couple of figures and number bricks, which quietly turns block play into story play. The box doubles as storage. DUPLO and Mega Bloks do not connect, so pick one system per household.

Buy two? One box to start. Add a second DUPLO set at Christmas rather than buying two identical boxes now.

Art supplies

Anything that goes in a hand needs a twin. Anything that goes on a table can be shared.

Crayola My First Palm Grip Crayons

Egg-shaped crayons a two-year-old holds in a whole fist, which is how two-year-olds hold things. They are washable, they do not snap, and they roll less than stick crayons so fewer end up under the fridge. Six colors per pack is plenty at this age; more colors just means more to pick up.

Buy two? Yes. One pack per twin, and neither will accept a red crayon as a substitute for the other red crayon.

Puzzles

Peg puzzles are the right difficulty at two. Buy different puzzles, not duplicates.

Melissa & Doug Animals Wooden Peg Puzzles (set of 3)

Farm, pets and ocean, each with six or so pegged pieces and a matching picture underneath so a two-year-old can self-check. Three puzzles in one gift is ideal for twins: each kid takes one, and the third is the one they trade for. The pegs are the fat kind that survive being used as hammers.

Buy two? No. A set of three different puzzles is better than two of the same, because twins finish a puzzle and immediately want the other one anyway.

Melissa & Doug Farm Animals Sound Puzzle

Same peg format, but each animal moos or clucks when its piece drops into place. The sound is a light sensor, so it also goes off when a twin lifts a piece to show their sibling, which they will do forty times. It is the puzzle that gets pulled out for guests, and a good one for the twin who is not yet interested in the quiet ones.

Buy two? No, one is plenty. Pair it with the silent set above so the twins are not both waiting on the same eight pieces.

Books

Two-year-olds have favorites and demand them nightly. Plan for that.

Little Blue Truck (board book)

Rhyming, animal noises on every page, and a plot a two-year-old can follow: the truck helps, gets stuck, and the animals help back. It reads aloud well to two kids because there are enough "beep" and "moo" cues that both can join in. The board edition is thick enough to be thrown at a sibling without damage to either.

Buy two? One copy, unless your twins are already at the stage of each wanting to "read" alone in a corner, in which case a second copy is cheap peace.

Llama Llama Red Pajama (board book)

The bedtime-worry story, which lands harder at two than at one because two-year-olds now understand what "Mama's coming" means. It is short and rhythmic, and the board edition trims the text to a length that works when you are reading to two kids who both want to turn the page. It also gives twin parents a useful line for the bedtime callbacks.

Buy two? No. One bedtime copy, read from the middle of the bed with a twin on each side.

Personalized keepsakes

Two names, equal size, every time.

Personalized name puzzle or step stool

At two, a wooden name puzzle is a real toy as well as a keepsake: the twins learn the letters of their own name by fitting them in, and they learn the other twin's name because it is right there next to theirs. Personalized step stools do the same job in the bathroom, where two-year-olds start washing their own hands. Either one gets kept.

Buy two? Yes, and identical except for the name. Personalized gifts are the one category where "matching" is exactly right.
