The one-each vs. one-to-share rule

At twelve months, twins do not share. They parallel-play, which means two of anything small and one of anything big. Toys they hold, chew, push or carry need a twin. Toys they climb into or ride in together can be a single gift. Each card below says which side of that line it falls on.

Push toys & walkers

New walkers want something to shove around the room. Two new walkers want two of them.

Radio Flyer Classic Walker Wagon

The wooden walker wagon is the one we would pick over a plastic activity walker if you can only get one type. It is heavy enough that a wobbly twelve-month-old cannot outrun it, the clicker resistance stops it shooting across hardwood, and the bin gets used to haul blocks and stuffed animals for the next two years. It also looks fine sitting in a living room, which matters when there are two of them.

Buy two? Yes. This is the toy twins fight over hardest at one, and the wagons nest side by side without taking much more space.

Ride-ons & the family wagon

The big-box gifts. Coordinate these among grandparents so nobody doubles up by accident.

Little Tikes Cozy Coupe

Most one-year-olds cannot foot-scoot a Cozy Coupe yet, so for the first few months this is a parent-pushed car with a kid inside. That is exactly why it works for twins: the rear push handle lets one adult drive one twin around the yard while the other waits a turn, and by eighteen months they drive themselves. The removable floor means it lasts to age four or five.

Buy two? If you have the space, yes, and pick two different colors so ownership is obvious. If not, one Coupe plus one of the ride-ons below gives each twin their own wheels.

Stacking, sorting & sensory

Small, cheap, and the fastest way to start a twin squabble if there is only one.

Fisher-Price Rock-a-Stack

The rings-on-a-wobbly-post toy has been around for decades because it does the job: one-year-olds pull the rings off, chew them, and eventually put them back in order. The plant-based version is the same toy with a nicer feel. It is the kind of thing twins hand each other and then immediately want back.

Buy two? Yes. It costs about what a card costs, and a matched pair means the rings can mix without anyone caring which post they came from.

Board books

Two on one lap at bedtime means the book has to be big, simple and sturdy.

Brown Bear, Brown Bear, What Do You See?

The repeating question-and-answer structure is perfect for reading to two at once because both kids can chime in on the animal names before they can say much else. The Eric Carle art is bold enough to read from across a crib. Get the padded or standard board book, not the paperback, because one-year-olds eat corners.

Buy two? One copy for the shared bedtime read is normal. Two copies is not silly if your twins each want to hold a book, and it cuts the tug-of-war at naptime.

Dear Zoo (lift-the-flap board book)

Lift-the-flap books are the first books one-year-olds operate themselves, and Dear Zoo is the one whose flaps survive longest. The story is short enough for two squirming kids, and the "he was too big, so I sent him back" refrain becomes a family catchphrase. Expect the elephant flap to go first.

Buy two? Yes, for this one. Flap books get torn, and twins take turns being the one who tears it, so a backup copy is worth having.

Bath toys

By one most twins bathe together, so bath toys are the rare small gift that really is shared.

Munchkin Fishin' Magnetic Bath Toy

A short fishing rod with a magnet and three bobbing sea creatures to catch. One-year-olds cannot fish yet; they hold the rod and a parent steers the fish onto it, and the delight is the same. The creatures double as pour-and-float toys, and the whole set dries on the tub edge without growing mildew inside.

Buy two? Yes, because there is only one rod per set and the rod is the whole toy. Two sets gives each twin a rod and six fish in the water.

Keepsakes & clothing size-ups

The practical gifts parents quietly hope for.

Personalized twin keepsake

Ornaments, name blocks, wooden name puzzles and framed prints made for two names are the gifts parents keep long after the toys are donated. If you go this route, make sure each twin's name gets equal billing and the same size lettering; a "big" name and a "little" name on a shared piece is the kind of thing a seven-year-old twin notices. Twin-specific makers on Amazon do this well.

Buy two? Either one shared piece with both names, or two matching individual pieces. Never one for one twin.

Pajamas and basics one size up

Twins turn one wearing 12-month clothes and wake up a month later needing 18-24 month. Two-piece cotton pajamas, plain tees and leggings in the next size are the most-used gift on this page, and the least glamorous. Ask the parents which size they are about to run out of, then buy that size, not the one the twins wear today.

Buy two? Yes, always. Matching is fine at this age if the parents like it; coordinating colors is the safe middle ground.
