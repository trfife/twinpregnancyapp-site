The first month with twins is intense, but it's survivable with a plan. The goal isn't perfection—it's keeping two babies fed, both of you rested in shifts, and your sanity intact. Here's what actually helps.

Run the night in shifts

Two newborns rarely sleep at the same time on their own, so split the night with your partner or a helper. One person sleeps deeply while the other handles both babies, then you swap.

Protect real sleep

The off-duty parent should sleep somewhere quiet. A white noise machine masks one baby crying so the resting parent banks uninterrupted hours.

Sync their schedules

Wake the second twin when the first wakes to eat. Feeding and settling them together is the single biggest time-saver in month one.

Feed both at once

A twin feeding pillow lets you bottle or nurse both babies simultaneously instead of back to back.

Track who ate and slept

With two babies you will lose track of who fed last by 3 a.m. A simple baby log or app keeps both schedules straight.

Set up a grab-and-go station

Stock a basket on each floor with diapers, wipes, burp cloths, and spare onesies so you're never carrying two babies upstairs for supplies.

Get hands free

Sometimes both babies want to be held. A twin-friendly carrier or two wraps let you soothe one (or both) while you eat or move around.

Accept help and lower the bar

Say yes to meals, laundry, and an extra set of hands. The first month is about triage, not tidiness—both babies fed and everyone safe is a win.
