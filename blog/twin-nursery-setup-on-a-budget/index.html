A twin nursery sounds twice as expensive, but it doesn't have to be. The trick is knowing what genuinely needs doubling, what two babies can share, and where to spend versus save. Here's a budget-minded plan.

Double only what matters: sleep

Safe sleep is the one place to buy two. Each baby needs their own surface—but those surfaces can be affordable.

Two budget cribs

Two simple convertible cribs cost less than one designer set and grow with the babies into toddler beds.

Mini cribs for small rooms

Tight on space? Two mini cribs fit where one full crib would and cost a fraction of the price.

View on Amazon

Share the big furniture

Most of the room can be shared, which is where the real savings live.

One dresser plus changer

Skip a separate changing table. A dresser with a clip-on changing pad on top serves both babies and stores clothes for two.

View on Amazon

One comfortable chair

You only need one nursing or rocking chair. Choose one wide enough to tandem feed both babies at once.

View on Amazon

Save on storage and organization

Two babies generate a lot of stuff. Affordable bins and shelving keep it under control without a custom closet.

  • Fabric bins labeled by size—twins blow through sizes fast
  • A rolling cart for diapers, wipes, and creams within arm's reach
  • Over-door organizers for the odds and ends

View on Amazon

Splurge on one shared sleep aid

If you spend anywhere, make it a single white noise machine. Many twin parents use one, and it's cheaper than two of anything else.

View on Amazon

Skip for now

Hold off on wipe warmers, two of every toy, and elaborate decor. Buy doubles only as you discover you actually need them—your budget and your closet will thank you.

Sources: AAP (HealthyChildren.org): How to Keep Your Sleeping Baby Safe.
