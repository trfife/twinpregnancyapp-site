Best car seats for twins

Buying car seats for twins is the one purchase you can't skip and can't delay—you need two installed and inspected before the hospital will discharge your babies. The good news: you don't need the most expensive seats on the shelf. You need two seats that fit your babies, fit each other side by side, and fit your actual car. Here's how to choose without second-guessing.

Start with two infant car seats

For the newborn months, most twin parents choose two infant (rear-facing, carrier-style) seats. They click out of a base so you can carry a sleeping baby inside, snap onto a compatible double stroller, and move one baby between cars without re-installing. With twins, that convenience is worth a lot—you'll be doing everything twice.

Look for a seat with a low minimum weight, since twins often arrive smaller and earlier than singletons. Many seats start around 4 pounds, which matters if one or both babies spend time in the NICU.

Two bases, two cars, or both?

If you have two drivers and two cars, consider an extra base for each seat so you're never re-installing in a parking lot with two crying babies. Bases are cheaper than whole seats and stay buckled in.

Will two seats fit across your back row?

This is the question that trips up twin parents. A standard back seat is roughly 50 inches wide, and two infant seats plus their bases can be a tight squeeze—especially in a sedan. Before you buy, measure the width of each seat at its widest point and add them together, then compare to your bench.

  • Two across, no third seat: Many infant seats fit side by side in the outboard positions. Test-fit if you can.
  • Two across plus an older child: If a sibling also needs a seat, you'll want narrow ("slim") seats and may need three across.
  • Check the buckles: Seats can technically fit but block each other's belt or LATCH anchors. Confirm you can actually buckle both.

When to switch to convertible seats

Infant seats are outgrown by height or weight, often between 9 and 18 months. From there, twins move to convertible seats that stay rear-facing far longer—a key safety win. For twins, the magic word is narrow. A slim convertible makes "three across" possible if you add a sibling, and keeps two seats from crowding each other.

Buy two identical convertibles

Matching seats install the same way and take the guesswork out of daily life. Once you've learned one, you've learned both—and you can swap accessories or covers between them.

Small extras that make twin car trips easier

  • Two car seat mirrors so you can glance at each rear-facing baby.
  • Window shades to keep afternoon sun off two faces at once.
  • Seat protectors under each seat—twins mean double the spills.

Get both seats checked

A free inspection by a Certified Child Passenger Safety Technician is the single best thing you can do. Many fire stations, hospitals, and health departments offer it. Bring both seats and your vehicle—techs will confirm the angle, the tightness, and that two seats coexist safely in your specific car. Do this before your due date, because twins love to arrive early.

A quick buying checklist

  • Two infant seats with a low minimum weight for smaller, earlier babies
  • Measured to fit side by side in your actual back row
  • Extra bases if you have two cars or two drivers
  • A plan to move to two narrow convertibles around 9–18 months
  • Both seats professionally inspected before discharge

Choose seats that fit your babies and your car, install them early, and get them checked. With two seats buckled and ready, one of the biggest "bringing twins home" worries is already handled.
