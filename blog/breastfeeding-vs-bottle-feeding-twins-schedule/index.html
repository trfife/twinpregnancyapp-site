If you've already weighed bottles versus breast, the next question is timing: how do you actually structure a day feeding two babies? The golden rule with twins is to feed them together. A shared schedule protects your rest and keeps both babies in rhythm.

The one rule: feed them together

Whether you nurse or bottle feed, wake the second twin when the first wakes to eat. Letting them drift onto separate schedules means you feed around the clock with no breaks, the fastest route to burnout.

A sample newborn day

Newborn twins typically eat every 2–3 hours. Here's a realistic rhythm you can adapt. The times are flexible; the spacing is the point.

  • 7:00 a.m. — Both wake and feed together
  • 9:30 a.m. — Feed, then a short awake window
  • 12:00 p.m. — Feed; nap both babies after
  • 2:30 p.m. — Feed together
  • 5:00 p.m. — Feed; fussy "witching" stretch is normal
  • 7:30 p.m. — Feed and begin a wind-down routine
  • 10:00 p.m. — Dream feed before the long stretch
  • Overnight — Feed on demand, both together

Bottle and combo timing

If you bottle or combo feed, pre-measure formula so a 3 a.m. feed takes seconds, not minutes. A formula dispenser keeps night feeds fast and quiet.

Adjust as they grow

Around 3–4 months, feeds space out and a longer night stretch appears. Keep them synced as the gaps widen and the whole day gets more predictable.
