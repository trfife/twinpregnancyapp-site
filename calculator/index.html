Why twins usually arrive earlier

A single-baby pregnancy is dated to 40 weeks, and that date is still the reference point for twins. But more than half of twins are born before 37 weeks, and even uncomplicated twin births are usually planned earlier than 40 weeks. The planned week depends on whether the babies share a placenta, and UK and US guidance don’t give the same weeks.

Your twin type matters

Two placentas (di/di): UK guidance offers planned birth at 37 weeks; US information citing ACOG says these twins are typically delivered at 38 weeks 0 days and not past 39 weeks. Shared placenta, two sacs (mono/di): UK guidance offers planned birth at 36 weeks; a 2026 US checklist plans birth between 34 weeks 0 days and 37 weeks 6 days. Shared placenta and sac (mono/mono): both plan a cesarean, UK between 32 weeks 0 days and 33 weeks 6 days, US between 32 weeks 0 days and 34 weeks 0 days. These weeks are only for uncomplicated pregnancies; complications such as twin-to-twin transfusion syndrome, growth problems or pre-eclampsia need their own date. Your care team sets your actual plan.

Sources: NICE NG137 (UK), SASGOG di/di twins (US), SMFM 2026 monochorionic checklists (US), ACOG preterm labor (US).
